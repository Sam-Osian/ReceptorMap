Methodology

How pKi is calculated and plotted

pKi = 9 − log₁₀(Ki), where Ki is the equilibrium inhibition constant in nM. Higher pKi indicates stronger binding. Where only a Ki range is available, the geometric mean of the lower and upper bounds is used. Pharmacological direction (left/right) is drawn from activity annotations where present; unannotated entries are plotted as "unknown" and do not imply a specific direction.

Limitations and caveats

Activity direction (agonist/antagonist) is annotated for a minority of entries; the remainder show binding affinity only. Ki values are drawn from published literature and curated databases — they reflect in-vitro binding, not in-vivo efficacy or clinical effect. Coverage is uneven across drug–receptor pairs. pKi should be interpreted in the context of receptor reserve, tissue distribution, and competing pharmacology. This tool is not a substitute for primary literature review or clinical judgement.

Data sources

Ki values are drawn primarily from the Guide to Pharmacology (GtoPdb) and supplementary literature sources. Source URLs and evidence notes are attached where available — these can be inspected in the raw dataset on the Dataset page. Drug scope is currently limited to antidepressants and selected related agents (antipsychotics, agomelatine). Coverage of additional drug classes is planned.
